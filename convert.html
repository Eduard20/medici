MEDICI · EXPERIMENTAL

From paper
to playback.

Turn printed sheet music into a MIDI you can hear on any instrument. Recognition runs on your device. Your PDF is never uploaded.

A first draft of the notes. This is experimental optical music recognition. It can misread pitches, rhythms, accidentals, voices, and repeats. Clear digital scores work best. Handwriting, tablature, lyrics-only pages, and complex layouts may fail. Compare the result with your score.

Up to 15 MB. Process 1–3 pages at a time. The original PDF is not saved.

Tempo is your choice; printed tempo changes and expressive timing are not reproduced. Keep this tab open while converting. A desktop or laptop is recommended.

First use downloads approximately 200–250 MB of engine and model files from jsDelivr and the official Partsong site. Models are cached locally when possible. No account, payment, or remote inference is used.

Recognition details & storage
Frees the model cache only. Your saved MIDI is unchanged. Models download again next time.

Open source, recognition limits & privacy
Based on Partsong by Paul Fremantle and homr. Integrated code is AGPL-3.0; complete source and modifications are available. Third-party notices remain in NOTICE.md. Music assets retain their separate rights.
The app downloads engine files, but sends no PDF, image, or recognized notes to those hosts. The hosts still receive ordinary request information such as your IP address. Source PDF pages are discarded when this page closes. Only MIDI you choose to use in the studio is saved in this browser. Browser data can be cleared or evicted.
Chrome/Edge with WebAssembly JSPI is the tested target. Other browsers are feature-detected; API support alone does not guarantee enough memory or full compatibility. Multithreaded recognition is not enabled here, so conversion can be slow. The homr model licensing FAQ notes uncertainty about training-data restrictions for commercial use. This is a noncommercial fan experiment.
